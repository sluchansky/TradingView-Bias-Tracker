TRADING BOT — GHOST SYSTEM GATE EFFECTIVENESS AUDIT

OBJECTIVE

Do NOT redesign the trading system.

Do NOT add a new strategy.

Do NOT change thresholds.

Do NOT loosen or tighten the decision gate.

Do NOT add VIX logic.

Do NOT change Databento ingestion.

Do NOT change execution behavior.

The purpose of this task is to use the ghost/shadow measurement infrastructure that was built in Phase 3.6.x to determine whether the existing decision gate is actually helping or hurting trading performance.

We are done making speculative changes.

From this point forward, a production trading-rule change must be supported by measured evidence.

PART 7 — CALCULATE “RULE VALUE”

For every decision-gate rule, calculate whether that rule appears to save or cost performance.

We need metrics such as:

Blocked Winners

Trades blocked by this rule that would have made positive R.

Blocked Losers

Trades blocked by this rule that would have lost.

Avoided Loss R

Total negative R prevented by the rule.

Missed Profit R

Total positive R rejected by the rule.

Net Gate Contribution

Calculate:

NET_GATE_VALUE_R = AVOIDED_LOSS_R - MISSED_PROFIT_R

Example:

If VWAP blocked:

* 8 losing setups totaling -8R
* 3 winning setups totaling +5R

Then:

NET_GATE_VALUE = +3R

VWAP was beneficial over that sample.

If a rule prevented 2R of losses but rejected 11R of winners:

NET_GATE_VALUE = -9R

That rule deserves investigation.

Do NOT automatically recommend removal based only on this number.

Sample size and uncertainty must also be shown.

PART 15 — SAMPLE-SIZE PROTECTION

Do not draw strong conclusions from tiny sample sizes.

Use the statistics infrastructure already built where possible.

For key metrics include:

* N
* bootstrap confidence interval for average R or expectancy
* uncertainty warning
* minimum sample threshold

Suggested classification:

* N < 10 = ANECDOTAL
* N 10–29 = EARLY
* N 30–99 = MODERATE
* N >= 100 = STRONGER EVIDENCE

These labels do not imply statistical proof.

They are simply protection against overreacting to tiny samples.
